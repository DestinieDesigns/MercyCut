MercyCut Web v2 — Movement Clips

Simple flow, no prompt, no captions needed: 1. Upload a video. 2. Pick the person to follow. 3. Find the best movement clips. 4. Tick the clips you want and press Save Picked Clips. Captions can be added somewhere else later if you want them — they are not needed here.

No Python. No install. No upload. Open this file in Chrome or Edge, pick a video from your computer, and edit it right in this tab. Your video never leaves this device — there is no server here to send it to.

Honest limits, please read: browsers cannot play every format (MP4 works best; MKV and some MOV files may not play). Export runs in real time, so a 60-second clip takes about 60 seconds to export, and you must keep this tab in front while it exports. For very long services, cut short clips first. The desktop version is still faster for 2–3 hour files.

Find Best Movement Clips — no captions, no transcript, no prompt

Movement means how far the person you picked moves — left, right, up, down — between samples. The app watches the whole video, measures movement in every 2-minute window, and picks the windows with the most movement. Singing, music, and quiet talking do not matter here, because we watch movement, not sound.

Steps: 1. Upload a video above. 2. In “Follow a person” below, click the person to follow (Person 1, 2, 3, or 4). If you do not pick one, it will pick the largest, most central person at the start — check it is the right person. 3. Press “Find Best Movement Clips” here. It will sample the video, find the best clips, and track the person in each one automatically. 4. When tracking hits 100%, the clip is ready to export — no Review click, no Approve click. Just tick the clips you want in My Clips and press “Save Picked Clips”, or press Export on a single clip. If a live Export comes out choppy (about 4 frames a second), press “Export Offline (smooth)” on that clip instead — it renders frame by frame at 30fps, slower to export, but smooth to watch.

Tracking speed & smoothness — click to change: Pick a preset in one tap, or change the three settings below it yourself. Change these before you press Find Best Movement Clips. If clips are already tracked, change them then press “Retry Tracking for Clip” to re-track with the new settings.

Fastest = fewest checks, quickest, crop may jump a bit. Fast & Smooth = the balanced default. Most Accurate = checks every 0.5 seconds at full size, slowest, most precise. Smoothest = very smooth crop, but may lag a little if the person walks fast. Export itself is still real-time in a browser — a 2-minute clip takes about 2 minutes to save — that part cannot be sped up here without a different export tool.

Refine what counts as a person (logos): Logos are usually small, in a corner, and never move. A real person is bigger and moves. Use these to stop logo hands being picked up as a person.

Autopilot — walk away

Press Start Autopilot, then walk away. It runs one clip at a time: it picks the person to follow (the one you already picked, or the largest, most central person if you have not picked one), tracks them, then makes captions with local Whisper. It does not download anything and does not approve clips. When you come back, tick the clips you want and press Save Picked Clips.

Keep this tab open and your computer awake. If you close the tab or the computer sleeps, autopilot stops where it is. Press Start again and it will skip clips that are already done.

Workflow: 1. Upload the video. 2. Auto-find looks for clips about 2 minutes long with the most activity in them — most talking, loudest, most continuous speech, not quiet or empty parts. 3. It clips those scenes and tracks the person. 4. Captions are added only if the box above is checked. 5. When you come back, tick the clips you want and press Save Picked Clips, or Save Picked Without Captions.

Autopilot is off. Add at least one clip, then press Start.

Tracking follows the detected person whose centre is nearest to the last position, within 60% of the video width, with at least 50% confidence. This is a best-effort position match; crowded or crossing people can still confuse it. If no match is close enough, it holds the last position and marks the sample lost. Manual keyframes can correct any sample.
